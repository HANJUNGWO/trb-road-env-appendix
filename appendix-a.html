Appendix A. VLM Prompt

This appendix provides the full prompt materials used for VLM-based road-environment assessment.

Appendix A.1 System Prompt

You are an expert in transportation engineering, road environment assessment, and human perception of urban streets.

Your task is to evaluate a driver-perspective street-view image captured from a moving vehicle.

Evaluate ONLY the visual information that is directly observable in the image.

Do NOT infer information that cannot be visually confirmed.

Do NOT estimate future traffic conditions.

Do NOT infer driver behavior or traffic safety beyond what is visually observable.

Ignore minor image quality issues such as slight blur or lighting differences unless they prevent evaluation of the visible road environment.

Evaluate each attribute independently.

A high or low score on one attribute should not automatically influence the scores assigned to the other attributes.

Return only the requested JSON object.

Appendix A.2 User Prompt

Please evaluate this driver-perspective street-view image.

Rate each attribute using a 7-point Likert scale.

1 = Very Low
2 = Low
3 = Slightly Low
4 = Neutral
5 = Slightly High
6 = High
7 = Very High

Evaluate the following five perceptual attributes independently.

1. Greenery

Evaluate the amount of visible vegetation in the driver's field of view.

Consider:
- trees
- grass
- shrubs
- roadside vegetation
- other visible natural landscape elements

Do not count artificial green colors, advertisements, signs, or painted surfaces as vegetation.

2. Openness

Evaluate how visually open and spacious the road environment appears.

Consider:
- visible sky
- long viewing distance
- roadway width
- spatial openness
- absence of strong visual enclosure

Low openness may be associated with dense roadside buildings, narrow streets, walls, tunnels, or other visually enclosed environments.

3. Visual Complexity

Evaluate the amount and diversity of visual information in the road scene.

Consider:
- buildings
- vehicles
- traffic signs
- lane markings
- roadside facilities
- advertisements
- intersections
- street furniture
- other visible objects and structures

Higher scores indicate a greater amount and diversity of visual information.

4. Perceived Safety

Evaluate how safe the road environment appears for driving based only on visible physical characteristics.

Consider:
- road geometry
- forward visibility
- lane organization
- roadside condition
- apparent traffic order
- visible obstacles

Do not infer actual crash risk or use information that is not visible in the image.

5. Driving Stress

Evaluate how visually stressful the road environment appears for a driver.

Consider:
- visual clutter
- dense or disordered traffic
- complicated intersections
- roadway complexity
- narrow roadway space
- roadside obstacles
- overall visual pressure

Higher scores indicate greater perceived driving stress.

Evaluate only what is visible in the image.

Do not infer unobserved traffic conditions, driver behavior, or actual accident risk.

Do not explain your reasoning.

Return ONLY valid JSON in the following format:

{
  "greenery": integer,
  "openness": integer,
  "visual_complexity": integer,
  "perceived_safety": integer,
  "driving_stress": integer
}

Appendix A.3 Expected JSON Output

{
  "greenery": integer,
  "openness": integer,
  "visual_complexity": integer,
  "perceived_safety": integer,
  "driving_stress": integer
}
