Appendix B. Human Survey Instrument

This appendix provides the questionnaire structure used for the human validation study, including the survey instructions, attribute definitions, response scale, and item format.

Survey Instruction

Instructions Shown to Participants

Please evaluate each road scene based only on what is directly visible in the image.

Respond according to your immediate visual impression as a driver.

There are no correct or incorrect answers.

Do not assume information that is not visible in the image, such as traffic conditions beyond the frame, neighborhood reputation, time-varying conditions, or prior familiarity with the area.

Evaluate each item independently. A scene may score high on one attribute and low on another.

Use the full 7-point response scale whenever appropriate.

Survey Structure

Questionnaire Overview

Participants were shown one road-scene image at a time and asked to rate the same set of five perceptual attributes for each image. All evaluations were based solely on visible image content and were intended to reflect immediate driver-oriented perception.

  • Platform: Google Forms
  • Stimuli: representative driver-perspective road-scene images
  • Response format: 7-point Likert scale
  • Evaluation unit: one image at a time
  • Common instruction applied to all images and all respondents
Response Scale

7-Point Likert Scale Used in the Survey

The same scale structure was applied across all five perceptual attributes.

Scale Point Verbal Anchor Interpretation
1 Very low The attribute is almost absent or extremely weak in the scene.
2 Low The attribute is present only to a limited degree.
3 Somewhat low The attribute is slightly present but remains below a neutral impression.
4 Neutral The scene appears moderate or neither low nor high on the attribute.
5 Somewhat high The attribute is meaningfully present above a neutral impression.
6 High The attribute is strongly present in the scene.
7 Very high The attribute is extremely prominent in the scene.
Attribute Definitions

Definitions Provided for Human Evaluation

1. Greenery

The extent to which vegetation such as trees, grass, shrubs, or planting is visibly present in the road scene.

2. Openness

The extent to which the road environment appears spatially open, broad, and visually unobstructed from the driver's perspective.

3. Complexity

The extent to which the scene appears visually dense, busy, cluttered, or compositionally complicated.

4. Safety

The extent to which the road environment appears safe, orderly, and comfortable from a driver's point of view.

5. Stress

The extent to which the road environment appears likely to induce tension, burden, or psychological strain while driving.

Question Format

Item Wording Used in the Survey

For each image, participants responded to the following five questions using the 7-point scale shown above.

Q1. Greenery
How high is the level of greenery visible in this road scene?
Q2. Openness
How open does this road scene appear from the driver's perspective?
Q3. Complexity
How visually complex does this road scene appear?
Q4. Safety
How safe does this road scene appear for a driver?
Q5. Stress
How stressful does this road scene appear for a driver?
Administration Notes

Survey Administration Notes

  • Each image was evaluated independently.
  • Participants were instructed to rely on direct visual impression rather than extended reasoning.
  • The same five items were repeated for every image in the validation set.
  • The image set was designed to cover different route-environment patterns identified in the analysis.
  • Human ratings were later compared with VLM-generated scores for validation analysis.
